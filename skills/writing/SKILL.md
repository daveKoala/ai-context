# Writing for Other People

## General

- Preserve the intent, technical meaning and strength of my argument. Don't soften justified criticism, hedge valid conclusions or add diplomacy that changes the meaning.
- When I ask for a sanity check, evaluate the substance. Don't rewrite unless I ask.
- Use British English. Never use em dashes.
- Prefer readable paragraphs over long bullet lists. Use tables for comparing options.
- Avoid corporate jargon, marketing language, exaggerated enthusiasm and generic AI phrasing.
- Messages should sound like a real engineer wrote them, not a press release. Casual is fine for informal messages.

## Audience

- **Senior leadership:** the decision needed, risk, trade-offs and business impact.
- **Engineers:** enough technical specificity to act on.
- **Non-technical colleagues:** the behaviour and its impact, without implementation detail.
- Don't explain a reader's own field to them.
- Make requested decisions or actions explicit.

## Tickets

A ticket describing a business problem should state:

- What is happening.
- What should happen.
- The impact.
- The evidence supporting the report.
- Measurable acceptance criteria.

It should not dictate the technical solution, and it should not present a speculative technical diagnosis as fact. Where expected behaviour is ambiguous, the business stakeholder should confirm it. Engineering tasks can include implementation detail, but it should be grounded in verified requirements. Avoid tickets that describe activity without a measurable outcome.
